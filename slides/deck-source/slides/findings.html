WHAT ELSE THE DATA SHOW

Materials, air quality and room size all change the risk

26–31 % O₂

Cabin materials still burn near habitat air

On Cygnus, fabric, cotton and PMMA all burned at 0.53–0.70 atm. Nomex did not ignite.

Saffire IV–VI, NASA NTRS 20240002981

+8,500 ppm

Burning plastic fouls the cabin air

Thick PMMA raised cabin CO₂ by 8,100–8,500 ppm and CO by about 320 ppm, 3 to 6 times more than fabric.

Saffire VI, Table 2

1.8 → 5.1 mm/s

The enclosure changes how fast fire spreads

The same fabric in nearly the same air spread almost 3 times faster as the duct around it shrank from 51 cm to 7.6 cm.

Saffire I–IV and BASS; Olson et al., Table 1

Smoke detection: SAME, SAME-R and SPICE ran 86 smoke tests and 469 soot-point settings, all at 21 % O₂ and 1 atm.

Three supporting findings. First, the only solid burns near habitat air come from the Saffire flights, not from PSI, and fabric and plastic burned. Second, a plastic fire is an air-quality emergency as much as a fire: carbon monoxide and carbon dioxide rise several times more than for fabric. Third, the same fabric spread almost three times faster in a small duct than a large one, so habitat layout matters, not only the atmosphere.
