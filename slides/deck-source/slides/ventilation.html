KEY FINDING

Cutting the ventilation may not put out a fire in an oxygen-rich habitat

4.5×

longer burn in still air at habitat conditions than in Earth cabin air (model: 25 s vs 5.5 s)

41 %

of habitat flames predicted still burning after 30 s with no air flow; in Earth air, about 0 %

Stopping air flow is a standard first step against a spacecraft fire. ACME BRE (PSI-20) saw the same: emulated solids kept burning without flow at raised O₂.

ACME CFI-G (PSI-159): 179 tests, 18–40 % O₂, 0.5–3 atm. Weibull survival model, tested on ISS days it never saw (concordance 0.75). Habitat value is interpolated: no tests at 25–34 % O₂.

This is the headline. In still air at normal oxygen, a microgravity flame starves itself and goes out within seconds. Every one of the 41 flames at or below 25 percent oxygen did. At 34 to 40 percent they kept burning; 30 of 91 were still alight when the crew cut the fuel. Our model puts the habitat at about four and a half times longer, with four in ten flames still burning after 30 seconds. Honest caveat: no test was run between 25 and 34 percent, exactly where the habitat sits, which is one of our recommendations.
